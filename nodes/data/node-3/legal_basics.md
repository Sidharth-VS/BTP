## Contract Law

A contract is a legally enforceable agreement between two or more parties that creates mutual obligations. 
For a contract to be valid, it must contain: offer, acceptance, consideration, mutual assent (meeting of 
the minds), capacity, and legality.

Offer and Acceptance: An offer is a proposal made by one party (the offeror) to another (the offeree), 
indicating a willingness to enter into a contract on certain terms. Acceptance is the offeree's agreement 
to those terms.

Consideration is something of value exchanged between the parties. It distinguishes a contract from a 
gift. Consideration can be money, goods, services, or a promise to perform or refrain from an action.

## Tort Law

A tort is a civil wrong that causes harm or injury, leading to legal liability. Unlike criminal law, 
tort law focuses on compensating the injured party rather than punishing the wrongdoer.

Negligence is the most common type of tort. To establish negligence, a plaintiff must prove:
1. Duty: The defendant owed a duty of care to the plaintiff
2. Breach: The defendant breached that duty
3. Causation: The breach caused the plaintiff's injury
4. Damages: The plaintiff suffered actual damages

Strict liability applies regardless of fault. Product liability cases often involve strict liability — 
manufacturers can be held liable for defective products even without proof of negligence.

## Intellectual Property

Intellectual property (IP) refers to creations of the mind — inventions, literary and artistic works, 
designs, and symbols. IP rights are protected by law through patents, copyright, trademarks, and trade secrets.

Patents grant inventors exclusive rights to their inventions for a limited period (typically 20 years) 
in exchange for public disclosure. There are utility patents, design patents, and plant patents.

Copyright protects original works of authorship — literary, artistic, musical, and software works. 
Copyright protection is automatic upon creation and lasts for the life of the author plus 70 years.

Trademarks protect brand names, logos, and slogans that distinguish goods or services. Unlike patents 
and copyrights, trademark protection can last indefinitely as long as the mark is used in commerce.

## Constitutional Law

The U.S. Constitution is the supreme law of the United States. The Bill of Rights (first 10 amendments) 
protects fundamental civil liberties — freedom of speech, religion, press, assembly, and petition 
(1st Amendment); right to bear arms (2nd Amendment); protections against unreasonable searches and 
seizures (4th Amendment); right against self-incrimination and double jeopardy (5th Amendment).

Due process (5th and 14th Amendments) requires that the government must respect all legal rights owed 
to a person according to the law. Substantive due process protects certain fundamental rights from 
government interference. Procedural due process ensures fair procedures.

## Criminal Law

Criminal law involves the prosecution by the government of a person for an act classified as a crime. 
Crimes are divided into felonies (serious crimes, typically punishable by more than one year in prison) 
and misdemeanors (less serious crimes).

The elements of a crime typically include: actus reus (the guilty act) and mens rea (the guilty mind 
or criminal intent). Both must be proven beyond a reasonable doubt for a conviction.

Criminal defenses include: self-defense, insanity defense, alibi, duress, entrapment, and statute of 
limitations. The Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule prevents illegally obtained evidence from being 
used in court.

## Data Privacy Law

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a comprehensive EU regulation on data privacy that 
applies to all organizations processing the personal data of EU residents. Key principles include:
- Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency
- Purpose limitation (data collected for specified, explicit purposes)
- Data minimization
- Accuracy
- Storage limitation
- Integrity and confidentiality

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) grants California residents rights over their personal 
information, including the right to know, right to delete, right to opt-out of sale, and right to 
non-discrimination.

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) establishes national standards for protecting 
sensitive patient health information (PHI). Covered entities must implement safeguards to ensure the 
confidentiality, integrity, and availability of PHI.

## Employment Law

Employment law governs the rights and obligations between employers and employees. Key areas include:
- Title VII of the Civil Rights Act: prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, 
  sex, or national origin
- ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act): prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities
- FLSA (Fair Labor Standards Act): establishes minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping requirements
- FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act): provides eligible employees with unpaid, job-protected leave

At-will employment (common in the U.S.) means either the employer or employee can terminate the 
employment relationship at any time, for any reason (with some exceptions), without liability.
